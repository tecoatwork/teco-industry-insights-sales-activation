## Intelligence report

Recommended sections: executive summary; consumer shifts; regulation and compliance; supply chain and innovation; competitor and brand activity; opportunity list; Top 3; SCRP; next watchlist; sources. Shorten the report when evidence is thin.

For each opportunity include target market, buyer type, product or solution, buyer impact, entry channel, timing, evidence, risk, barrier and validation action. Rank by value, urgency, evidence strength and supplier executability.

## Cold email

Use one verified trigger and one buyer problem. By default provide three short subject lines, preview text, body and one low-friction CTA. Explain what changed, why it matters and what to check next. Never pretend an industry trend is a fact about the recipient.

## LinkedIn message

Use for a connected contact or a person with a public trigger. Keep the first message short, specific and answerable. Use SCRP's Situation and Complication for relevance, one useful Resolution idea, and one proof point. Do not deliver a full company pitch in the first message.

## WhatsApp message

Draft only for a legitimate existing contact context or an explicitly authorized scenario. State identity and contact context, give one relevant change and ask one answerable question. Do not design unsolicited bulk messaging.

## LinkedIn post

Cover one decision-level insight. Start with a truthful risk, number or counterintuitive fact; explain the change, mechanism, buyer checks and scope boundary; close with one genuine question and at most one soft CTA. Put external links in a “First comment sources” block and use three to five precise hashtags. By default offer two distinct angles and recommend one.

## Information separation

An internal report may contain capability matching and unknowns. Customer-facing content may use only information appropriate for public or one-to-one disclosure. Exclude internal costs, customer lists, unreleased products, unverified capabilities and confidential material.
